METHODOLOGY

A. Architectural Design and Multi-Scale Feature Extraction

Let x in R^(C x T) denote a window of C=32 scalp EEG channels over T time samples. MRANC maps x through a shared encoder composed of length-preserving 1D convolutional blocks with batch normalization and GELU activations, producing a latent feature tensor F in R^(C_f x T) with C_f=128. On top of F we attach a Multi-Scale Attention Block (MSAB) implemented as a residual adapter. Three parallel branches apply depthwise-separable convolutions along time. Each branch performs a grouped depthwise convolution with groups=C_f (one filter per channel) followed by a pointwise 1x1 convolution. Kernel sizes are 5, 11, and 21 samples, yielding receptive fields of approximately 25 ms, 55 ms, and 105 ms at 200 Hz (and proportionally shorter at 128 Hz DEAP windows). Odd symmetric padding preserves temporal length. Branch outputs are summed and fused with a 1x1 convolution to obtain a multi-scale representation H.

Depthwise separable factorization reduces parameter count relative to dense convolutions while allowing each channel to maintain its own temporal filter bank. The multi-kernel design explicitly encodes the frequency-time uncertainty of EEG artifacts: short kernels capture rapid eye-blink transients and EMG bursts; long kernels capture slower baseline wander and sustained contamination. After fusion, a one-dimensional Squeeze-and-Excitation (SE) sub-network performs global average pooling across time, producing a channel descriptor z in R^(C_f). A lightweight bottleneck MLP (C_f -> C_f/r -> C_f, reduction ratio r=16) with sigmoid activation yields channel gates g in (0,1)^(C_f). The gated tensor H' = H ⊙ g is projected through a final 1x1 convolution and added to F as a residual: F_out = F + Conv1x1(H'). All weights in the final projection are initialized to zero so that F_out = F at training onset, preserving stacked pre-training and preventing catastrophic forgetting when clinical fine-tuning begins.

B. Sequential Knowledge-Stacking Pipeline

MRANC is not trained monolithically on a single corpus. Instead we employ Sequential Knowledge-Stacking, a progressive curriculum in which model weights are transferred across heterogeneous domains. Stage 1 establishes artifact sensitivity on the SSVEP Artifact Benchmark (physics-only losses, isolated checkpoint). Stage 2 fine-tunes on DEAP (32 channels, T=256, supervised hardware-reference alignment with polarity-aware correlation losses). Stage 3 continues on SEED (T=1000, 200 Hz) with matched montage and variance scaling. Stage 4 applies unsupervised domain adaptation on CHB-MIT clinical windows (T=1000): only mix.npy is available; optimization uses orthogonality among stems, total-variation regularization on artifact manifolds, and minimal cleaning penalties without reference wires.

Each stage resumes from the best checkpoint of the prior stage via model_state_dict loading. Clinical and benchmark stages reset optimizer state to avoid momentum mismatch across domains. This stacking order supplies generic artifact structure first, then affective EEG statistics, then long-window temporal context, and finally real seizure-adjacent clinical noise. The MSAB adapter is inserted after the encoder for the final clinical phase: Phase A freezes the encoder and four StemDecoder heads while training only the adapter for 15 epochs (Adam, learning rate 1e-5) with feature distillation MSE between attended and frozen encoder features plus weighted clinical physics losses. Phase B unfreezes the full network for 5 epochs at learning rate 1e-6 to harmonize representations. Final weights are stored in weights/mranc_final_attention.pth.

C. Explainable Residual Attention and Recalibration

Explainability is provided without altering the physics-locked output algebra: pred_eeg = mix - (pred_eog + pred_emg + pred_ecg + pred_basenoise). The MSAB exposes self.latest_attention_weights during inference. After SE gating, we compute a channel-recalibrated magnitude map, reduce across channels, and min-max normalize per batch element to produce a temporal saliency trace in [0,1] with shape (1, T). If downstream striding or pooling ever reduces temporal resolution, weights are upsampled with linear interpolation to match the raw input length before visualization.

For publication diagnostics we generate two figures at 300 DPI. Figure 1 (Denoising Fidelity) compares raw noisy channels, optional ground-truth or reference-proxy clean traces, and MRANC denoised outputs alongside spatial RMS envelopes to demonstrate amplitude preservation. Figure 2 (Attention Map Heatmap) overlays the spatial-mean EEG trace with the normalized attention heatmap, enabling visual correlation between attention peaks and ocular or muscular transients. Zero initialization of the adapter projection guarantees that explainability modules begin neutral: attention maps evolve only as the adapter learns clinically meaningful residuals, while stacked DEAP and SEED representations remain intact at step zero.

Stem decoders consume F_out and emit four 32-channel artifact estimates. Hardware reference projection heads map stems to 2/2/1 reference dimensions for supervised loss evaluation only; they are never fed into forward inference. The full forward pass therefore remains causally valid for deployment: only raw scalp EEG enters the network, and all interpretability signals are derived from internal attention state without requiring post-hoc gradient methods.
