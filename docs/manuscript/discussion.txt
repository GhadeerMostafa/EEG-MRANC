[subsection] Clinical Significance of Stem-Wise Decomposition

Neurologists reason about EEG in terms of spatial distribution, morphology, and evolution over time. By exporting four labeled artifact stems plus an explicit cleaned trace, MRANC mirrors clinical mental models. When ocular activity is isolated on Fp1 and adjacent frontopolar derivations, the reader can verify that frontal slowing is not misattributed to eye movement. When EMG bursts are sequestered on temporal and central derivations, muscle contamination during agitation is less likely to be mistaken for epileptiform sharp waves. Cardiac pickup separated into the ECG stem reduces false confidence in rhythmic activity that actually reflects pulse artifact in referential montages.

This stem-wise transparency supports quality control dashboards, seizure-detection pipelines that down-weight intervals flagged by attention and EMG stems, and tele-EEG review platforms that display cleaner traces alongside artifact timelines for remote attendings. None of these integrations are well served by monolithic denoising that returns only a cleaned waveform.

[subsection] Explainable AI in Regulated Neuro-Monitoring

Explainable artificial intelligence (XAI) is often treated as an optional visualization layer. In clinical neuro-engineering, it is a safety requirement. Post-hoc saliency methods applied to black-box denoisers can produce maps that are not tied to the generative mechanisms inside the model. MRANC embeds explainability in the forward pass: the Multi-Scale Attention Block produces normalized attention weights derived from the same features that drive stem decoders, displayed on the identical time base as the EEG. When attention elevates at the moment of a blink on Fp1, the clinician sees a coherent story: ocular stem activation, attention elevation, and smoothing of the denoised trace.

[subsection] Physics-Locked Decomposition vs Unconstrained Denoisers

Unconstrained time-frequency mapping models such as DTP-Net learn a direct noisy-to-clean transformation without enforcing an additive decomposition identity. Even when such models minimize reconstruction error, they may hallucinate non-physical neural content because nothing in the objective forbids inventing energy that was never present in the mixed recording. MRANC instead treats cleaning as constrained source separation: the cleaned trace is defined algebraically as the mixed signal minus the sum of four named artifact stems. This hard mathematical constraint eliminates signal hallucination risks inherent to black-box encoders and provides an auditable partition of every sample into neural plus artifact constituents.

[subsection] Information Conservation and Spectral Trust

The exact reconstruction identity provides a formal contract: the mixed signal equals the cleaned EEG plus the sum of artifacts (within numerical tolerance). High alpha/beta PSD correlation further assures that denoising did not blunt clinically relevant rhythms. Interpretation of SNR metrics requires care: SNR improvement measures relative variance redistribution after decomposition, not subjective quality alone. Reporting SNR alongside PSD correlation and stem visualizations mitigates single-metric misreadings.

While the MRANC framework demonstrates exceptional signal fidelity and robust generalization across multiple benchmark datasets, a realistic evaluation highlights specific architectural trade-offs and boundaries of operation.

[subsection] Algorithmic Complexity and Computational Overhead

The proposed four-stage sequential knowledge-stacking architecture, integrated with localized attention mechanisms and squeeze-and-excitation gating, introduces a higher computational footprint during the training phase compared to standard end-to-end networks like vanilla U-Nets or GANs. The multi-stage optimization requires sequential gradient tracking, making training from scratch resource-intensive. However, this overhead is heavily front-loaded; the inference phase remains highly efficient and viable for real-time deployment. Furthermore, by making pre-trained clinical model checkpoints available to external researchers on reasonable request (see Data Availability Statement), we minimize deployment friction, allowing collaborators to utilize the optimized baseline without undergoing the full computational training cycle.

[subsection] Hyperparameter Sensitivity in Multi-Stage Optimization

Enforcing the strict physics-locked identity constraint requires precise balancing across the multi-channel decomposition stems. Because the architecture relies on sequential curriculum learning, the optimization trajectory is sensitive to the initial hyperparameter configurations established in the early benchmark training stages. Transitioning the MRANC framework to entirely distinct physiological modalities (e.g., surface electromyography or electrocardiography) would require systemic recalibration of these gating thresholds.

[subsection] Clinical Edge Cases and Out-of-Distribution Dynamics

Although MRANC yields near-perfect evaluation metrics across the CHB-MIT, SEED, DEAP, and Artifact Benchmark datasets under a strict subject-independent validation protocol, performance boundaries in extreme, uncurated clinical environments remain an area for active investigation. Out-of-distribution dynamics—such as severe, high-amplitude motor spasms occurring simultaneously during an ictal event, or extreme variations in electrode-tissue impedance—present complex signal profiles. While the hardcoded additive reconstruction identity prevents the network from hallucinating artificial data structures during these anomalies, future clinical trials are required to fully map decomposition behavior under compound, real-world physiological distortions.

This study focuses on 32-channel, windowed preprocessing compatible with the stacked training pipeline. Clinical adaptation and evaluation use the CHB-MIT Scalp EEG Database (PhysioNet, patient chb01) only; broader multi-site clinical corpora were outside the scope of this release. Real-time causal operation and heterogeneous montages beyond the DEAP layout also remain open. Future work will integrate MRANC into end-to-end seizure detection benchmarks [3] and compare against ICA-LABEL and EEGdenoiseNet [2] under matched information-conservation constraints. Longer-term scaling vectors may additionally explore discrete EEG tokenization and multi-dataset foundation architectures, albeit at increased computational cost.
