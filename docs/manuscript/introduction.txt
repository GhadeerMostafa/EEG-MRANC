Scalp EEG records the superposition of neural field activity and non-cerebral interference. In routine clinical practice, neurologists must distinguish pathological rhythms from contamination due to eye movements, facial muscle activity, cardiac pulsation, electrode motion, and environmental noise. Failure to resolve this mixture can mimic spike activity, obscure slowing, or inflate event rates in quantitative monitoring systems. Robust preprocessing is therefore a prerequisite for reliable diagnosis, seizure detection, and longitudinal comparison across sessions.

Related Works and Limitations of Conventional Cleaning

Independent Component Analysis (ICA) and its automated variants remain the de facto standard for offline artifact rejection. ICA assumes statistical independence of sources and requires expert review or heuristic classifiers to label components as neural versus artifactual. In intensive-care and ambulatory settings, where channel count, impedance, and patient motion vary widely, manual component selection does not scale and introduces inter-rater variability. Moreover, removing components is inherently subtractive and can excise neural variance that shares statistical structure with ocular or muscular sources.

Regression-based filters (e.g., EOG/ECG regression) exploit auxiliary reference channels when available. Clinical long-term recordings often lack reliable references; bipolar montages and average references do not provide the clean regressors assumed by classical formulations. Regression also risks over-subtraction when artifact and neural signals are correlated across channels, producing amplitude flattening in frontal and temporal chains.

Black-box deep denoisers, including convolutional autoencoders and supervised architectures such as EEGdenoiseNet, have demonstrated impressive noise reduction on curated benchmarks. These models typically map noisy input to a cleaned output without enforcing conservation of signal energy or decomposing artifacts into physiologically named constituents. When the mapping is unconstrained, networks may learn dataset-specific shortcuts while offering no transparent account of what was removed. Such behavior is poorly aligned with regulatory expectations for clinical decision support.

Positioning of MRANC

MRANC addresses these gaps through three coupled design principles. First, a physics-locked decomposition algebra defines the cleaned EEG as the mixed signal minus the sum of four artifact stems, guaranteeing information conservation. Second, Sequential Knowledge-Stacking orders training from a controlled artifact benchmark through DEAP and SEED before unsupervised adaptation on CHB-MIT clinical windows. Third, a zero-initialized Multi-Scale Attention Block supplies temporal saliency maps that highlight contamination intervals without destabilizing stacked representations at initialization. Relative to ICA, MRANC produces channel-resolved artifact traces; relative to regression filters, it operates when reference wires are absent; relative to EEGdenoiseNet-style encoders, it yields named stems and attention-aligned explanations.
