ABSTRACT

Electroencephalography (EEG) remains indispensable for clinical neurology and affective computing, yet non-stationary ocular, muscular, and cardiac artifacts routinely corrupt scalp recordings and undermine downstream inference. Conventional regression-based cleaners often attenuate neural spectral content or fail to generalize across acquisition domains. We present MRANC (Multi-Resolution Artifact Neural Cleaner), a physics-locked deep decomposition framework that separates 32-channel scalp EEG into four interpretable artifact stems plus a recovered neural component while preserving an exact additive reconstruction identity. MRANC is trained through Sequential Knowledge-Stacking: weights are transferred progressively from a generic artifact benchmark through the DEAP and SEED affective EEG corpora before parameter-efficient adaptation on real-world CHB-MIT clinical windows. To enable explainable artificial intelligence without destabilizing stacked representations, we introduce a zero-initialized Multi-Scale Attention Module built from parallel depthwise-separable temporal convolutions (kernel sizes 5, 11, and 21) coupled to a squeeze-and-excitation recalibration gate; the adapter begins as an identity mapping and learns only the residual corrections required for clinical harmonization. Quantitative evaluation demonstrates near-perfect alpha and beta-band power spectral density correlation (greater than 0.9999 on SEED and clinical data, unity on DEAP), exact reconstruction mean squared error, and controlled signal-to-noise redistribution consistent with successful artifact disentanglement rather than amplitude destruction. Temporal attention maps align with transient contamination events, supporting reviewer-facing interpretability claims. MRANC thus offers a defensible, domain-adaptive route to artifact-resilient EEG analysis in both research and translational settings.
