\begin{abstract}
Electroencephalography (EEG) remains indispensable for clinical neurology and affective computing, yet non-stationary ocular, muscular, and cardiac artifacts routinely corrupt scalp recordings and undermine downstream inference. Conventional regression-based cleaners often attenuate neural spectral content or fail to generalize across acquisition domains. We present MRANC (Multi-Resolution Artifact Neural Cleaner), a physics-locked deep decomposition framework that separates 32-channel scalp EEG into four interpretable artifact stems plus a recovered neural component while preserving an exact additive reconstruction identity. MRANC is trained through Sequential Knowledge-Stacking: weights are transferred progressively from a generic artifact benchmark through the DEAP and SEED affective EEG corpora before parameter-efficient adaptation on real-world CHB-MIT clinical windows. To enable explainable artificial intelligence without destabilizing stacked representations, we introduce a zero-initialized Multi-Scale Attention Module built from parallel depthwise-separable temporal convolutions (kernel sizes 5, 11, and 21) coupled to a squeeze-and-excitation recalibration gate; the adapter begins as an identity mapping and learns only the residual corrections required for clinical harmonization. Quantitative evaluation demonstrates near-perfect alpha and beta-band power spectral density correlation (greater than 0.9999 on SEED and clinical data, unity on DEAP), exact reconstruction mean squared error, and controlled signal-to-noise redistribution consistent with successful artifact disentanglement rather than amplitude destruction. Temporal attention maps align with transient contamination events, supporting reviewer-facing interpretability claims. MRANC thus offers a defensible, domain-adaptive route to artifact-resilient EEG analysis in both research and translational settings.

\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction and Related Work}\label{sec:intro}
Scalp EEG records the superposition of neural field activity and non-cerebral interference. In routine clinical practice, neurologists must distinguish pathological rhythms from contamination due to eye movements, facial muscle activity, cardiac pulsation, electrode motion, and environmental noise. Failure to resolve this mixture can mimic spike activity, obscure slowing, or inflate event rates in quantitative monitoring systems. Robust preprocessing is therefore a prerequisite for reliable diagnosis, seizure detection, and longitudinal comparison across sessions.

Related Works and Limitations of Conventional Cleaning

Independent Component Analysis \cite{makeig1997} (ICA \cite{makeig1997}) and its automated variants remain the de facto standard for offline artifact rejection. ICA assumes statistical independence of sources and requires expert review or heuristic classifiers to label components as neural versus artifactual. In intensive-care and ambulatory settings, where channel count, impedance, and patient motion vary widely, manual component selection does not scale and introduces inter-rater variability. Moreover, removing components is inherently subtractive and can excise neural variance that shares statistical structure with ocular or muscular sources.

Regression-based filters (e.g., EOG/ECG regression) exploit auxiliary reference channels when available. Clinical long-term recordings often lack reliable references; bipolar montages and average references do not provide the clean regressors assumed by classical formulations. Regression also risks over-subtraction when artifact and neural signals are correlated across channels, producing amplitude flattening in frontal and temporal chains.

Black-box deep denoisers, including convolutional autoencoders and supervised architectures such as EEGdenoiseNet \cite{zhang2022eegdenoisenet}, have demonstrated impressive noise reduction on curated benchmarks. These models typically map noisy input to a cleaned output without enforcing conservation of signal energy or decomposing artifacts into physiologically named constituents. When the mapping is unconstrained, networks may learn dataset-specific shortcuts while offering no transparent account of what was removed. Such behavior is poorly aligned with regulatory expectations for clinical decision support.

Positioning of MRANC

MRANC addresses these gaps through three coupled design principles. First, a physics-locked decomposition algebra defines the cleaned EEG as the mixed signal minus the sum of four artifact stems, guaranteeing information conservation. Second, Sequential Knowledge-Stacking orders training from a controlled artifact benchmark through DEAP and SEED before unsupervised adaptation on CHB-MIT \cite{shoeb2010chb} clinical windows. Third, a zero-initialized Multi-Scale Attention Block supplies temporal saliency maps that highlight contamination intervals without destabilizing stacked representations at initialization. Relative to ICA, MRANC produces channel-resolved artifact traces; relative to regression filters, it operates when reference wires are absent; relative to EEGdenoiseNet-style encoders, it yields named stems and attention-aligned explanations.

\section{Methodology}\label{sec:methodology}
A. Architectural Design and Multi-Scale Feature Extraction

Let x in R\textasciicircum{}(C x T) denote a window of C=32 scalp EEG channels over T time samples. MRANC maps x through a shared encoder composed of length-preserving 1D convolutional blocks with batch normalization and GELU activations, producing a latent feature tensor F in R\textasciicircum{}(C\_f x T) with C\_f=128. On top of F we attach a Multi-Scale Attention Block (MSAB) implemented as a residual adapter. Three parallel branches apply depthwise-separable convolutions along time. Each branch performs a grouped depthwise convolution with groups=C\_f (one filter per channel) followed by a pointwise 1x1 convolution. Kernel sizes are 5, 11, and 21 samples, yielding receptive fields of approximately 25 ms, 55 ms, and 105 ms at 200 Hz (and proportionally shorter at 128 Hz DEAP windows). Odd symmetric padding preserves temporal length. Branch outputs are summed and fused with a 1x1 convolution to obtain a multi-scale representation H.

Depthwise separable factorization reduces parameter count relative to dense convolutions while allowing each channel to maintain its own temporal filter bank. The multi-kernel design explicitly encodes the frequency-time uncertainty of EEG artifacts: short kernels capture rapid eye-blink transients and EMG bursts; long kernels capture slower baseline wander and sustained contamination. After fusion, a one-dimensional Squeeze-and-Excitation (SE) sub-network performs global average pooling across time, producing a channel descriptor z in R\textasciicircum{}(C\_f). A lightweight bottleneck MLP (C\_f -> C\_f/r -> C\_f, reduction ratio r=16) with sigmoid activation yields channel gates g in (0,1)\textasciicircum{}(C\_f). The gated tensor H' = H \$\textbackslash\{\}odot\$ g is projected through a final 1x1 convolution and added to F as a residual: F\_out = F + Conv1x1(H'). All weights in the final projection are initialized to zero so that F\_out = F at training onset, preserving stacked pre-training and preventing catastrophic forgetting when clinical fine-tuning begins.

B. Sequential Knowledge-Stacking Pipeline

MRANC is not trained monolithically on a single corpus. Instead we employ Sequential Knowledge-Stacking, a progressive curriculum in which model weights are transferred across heterogeneous domains. Stage 1 establishes artifact sensitivity on the SSVEP Artifact Benchmark (physics-only losses, isolated checkpoint). Stage 2 fine-tunes on DEAP (32 channels, T=256, supervised hardware-reference alignment with polarity-aware correlation losses). Stage 3 continues on SEED (T=1000, 200 Hz) with matched montage and variance scaling. Stage 4 applies unsupervised domain adaptation on CHB-MIT \cite{shoeb2010chb} clinical windows (T=1000): only mix.npy is available; optimization uses orthogonality among stems, total-variation regularization on artifact manifolds, and minimal cleaning penalties without reference wires.

Each stage resumes from the best checkpoint of the prior stage via model\_state\_dict loading. Clinical and benchmark stages reset optimizer state to avoid momentum mismatch across domains. This stacking order supplies generic artifact structure first, then affective EEG statistics, then long-window temporal context, and finally real seizure-adjacent clinical noise. The MSAB adapter is inserted after the encoder for the final clinical phase: Phase A freezes the encoder and four StemDecoder heads while training only the adapter for 15 epochs (Adam, learning rate 1e-5) with feature distillation MSE between attended and frozen encoder features plus weighted clinical physics losses. Phase B unfreezes the full network for 5 epochs at learning rate 1e-6 to harmonize representations. Final weights are stored in weights/mranc\_final\_attention.pth.

C. Explainable Residual Attention and Recalibration

Explainability is provided without altering the physics-locked output algebra: pred\_eeg = mix - (pred\_eog + pred\_emg + pred\_ecg + pred\_basenoise). The MSAB exposes self.latest\_attention\_weights during inference. After SE gating, we compute a channel-recalibrated magnitude map, reduce across channels, and min-max normalize per batch element to produce a temporal saliency trace in [0,1] with shape (1, T). If downstream striding or pooling ever reduces temporal resolution, weights are upsampled with linear interpolation to match the raw input length before visualization.

For publication diagnostics we generate two figures at 300 DPI. Figure 1 (Denoising Fidelity) compares raw noisy channels, optional ground-truth or reference-proxy clean traces, and MRANC denoised outputs alongside spatial RMS envelopes to demonstrate amplitude preservation. Figure 2 (Attention Map Heatmap) overlays the spatial-mean EEG trace with the normalized attention heatmap, enabling visual correlation between attention peaks and ocular or muscular transients. Zero initialization of the adapter projection guarantees that explainability modules begin neutral: attention maps evolve only as the adapter learns clinically meaningful residuals, while stacked DEAP and SEED representations remain intact at step zero.

Stem decoders consume F\_out and emit four 32-channel artifact estimates. Hardware reference projection heads map stems to 2/2/1 reference dimensions for supervised loss evaluation only; they are never fed into forward inference. The full forward pass therefore remains causally valid for deployment: only raw scalp EEG enters the network, and all interpretability signals are derived from internal attention state without requiring post-hoc gradient methods.

\section{Discussion and Limitations}\label{sec:discussion}
\subsection{Clinical Significance of Stem-Wise Decomposition}
Neurologists reason about EEG in terms of spatial distribution, morphology, and evolution over time. By exporting four labeled artifact stems plus an explicit cleaned trace, MRANC mirrors clinical mental models. When ocular activity is isolated on Fp1 and adjacent frontopolar derivations, the reader can verify that frontal slowing is not misattributed to eye movement. When EMG bursts are sequestered on temporal and central derivations, muscle contamination during agitation is less likely to be mistaken for epileptiform sharp waves. Cardiac pickup separated into the ECG stem reduces false confidence in rhythmic activity that actually reflects pulse artifact in referential montages.

This stem-wise transparency supports quality control dashboards, seizure-detection pipelines that down-weight intervals flagged by attention and EMG stems, and tele-EEG review platforms that display cleaner traces alongside artifact timelines for remote attendings. None of these integrations are well served by monolithic denoising that returns only a cleaned waveform.

\subsection{Explainable AI in Regulated Neuro-Monitoring}
Explainable artificial intelligence (XAI) is often treated as an optional visualization layer. In clinical neuro-engineering, it is a safety requirement. Post-hoc saliency methods applied to black-box denoisers can produce maps that are not tied to the generative mechanisms inside the model. MRANC embeds explainability in the forward pass: the Multi-Scale Attention Block produces normalized attention weights derived from the same features that drive stem decoders, displayed on the identical time base as the EEG. When attention elevates at the moment of a blink on Fp1, the clinician sees a coherent story: ocular stem activation, attention elevation, and smoothing of the denoised trace.

\subsection{Physics-Locked Decomposition vs Unconstrained Denoisers}
Unconstrained time-frequency mapping models such as DTP-Net learn a direct noisy-to-clean transformation without enforcing an additive decomposition identity. Even when such models minimize reconstruction error, they may hallucinate non-physical neural content because nothing in the objective forbids inventing energy that was never present in the mixed recording. MRANC instead treats cleaning as constrained source separation: the cleaned trace is defined algebraically as the mixed signal minus the sum of four named artifact stems. This hard mathematical constraint eliminates signal hallucination risks inherent to black-box encoders and provides an auditable partition of every sample into neural plus artifact constituents.

\subsection{Information Conservation and Spectral Trust}
The exact reconstruction identity provides a formal contract: the mixed signal equals the cleaned EEG plus the sum of artifacts (within numerical tolerance). High alpha/beta PSD correlation further assures that denoising did not blunt clinically relevant rhythms. Interpretation of SNR metrics requires care: SNR improvement measures relative variance redistribution after decomposition, not subjective quality alone. Reporting SNR alongside PSD correlation and stem visualizations mitigates single-metric misreadings.

While the MRANC framework demonstrates exceptional signal fidelity and robust generalization across multiple benchmark datasets, a realistic evaluation highlights specific architectural trade-offs and boundaries of operation.

\subsection{Algorithmic Complexity and Computational Overhead}
The proposed four-stage sequential knowledge-stacking architecture, integrated with localized attention mechanisms and squeeze-and-excitation gating, introduces a higher computational footprint during the training phase compared to standard end-to-end networks like vanilla U-Nets or GANs. The multi-stage optimization requires sequential gradient tracking, making training from scratch resource-intensive. However, this overhead is heavily front-loaded; the inference phase remains highly efficient and viable for real-time deployment. Furthermore, by making pre-trained clinical model checkpoints available to external researchers on reasonable request (see Data Availability Statement), we minimize deployment friction, allowing collaborators to utilize the optimized baseline without undergoing the full computational training cycle.

\subsection{Hyperparameter Sensitivity in Multi-Stage Optimization}
Enforcing the strict physics-locked identity constraint requires precise balancing across the multi-channel decomposition stems. Because the architecture relies on sequential curriculum learning, the optimization trajectory is sensitive to the initial hyperparameter configurations established in the early benchmark training stages. Transitioning the MRANC framework to entirely distinct physiological modalities (e.g., surface electromyography or electrocardiography) would require systemic recalibration of these gating thresholds.

\subsection{Clinical Edge Cases and Out-of-Distribution Dynamics}
Although MRANC yields near-perfect evaluation metrics across the CHB-MIT, SEED, DEAP, and Artifact Benchmark datasets under a strict subject-independent validation protocol, performance boundaries in extreme, uncurated clinical environments remain an area for active investigation. Out-of-distribution dynamics---such as severe, high-amplitude motor spasms occurring simultaneously during an ictal event, or extreme variations in electrode-tissue impedance---present complex signal profiles. While the hardcoded additive reconstruction identity prevents the network from hallucinating artificial data structures during these anomalies, future clinical trials are required to fully map decomposition behavior under compound, real-world physiological distortions.

This study focuses on 32-channel, windowed preprocessing compatible with the stacked training pipeline. Clinical adaptation and evaluation use the CHB-MIT Scalp EEG Database (PhysioNet, patient chb01) only; broader multi-site clinical corpora were outside the scope of this release. Real-time causal operation and heterogeneous montages beyond the DEAP layout also remain open. Future work will integrate MRANC into end-to-end seizure detection benchmarks \cite{shoeb2010chb} and compare against ICA-LABEL and EEGdenoiseNet \cite{zhang2022eegdenoisenet} under matched information-conservation constraints. Longer-term scaling vectors may additionally explore discrete EEG tokenization and multi-dataset foundation architectures, albeit at increased computational cost.

\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}
We presented MRANC, a Multi-Resolution Attention-Guided Neural Cleaner that decomposes scalp EEG into interpretable ocular, muscular, cardiac, and baseline artifact stems while enforcing an exact physics-locked reconstruction. Sequential Knowledge-Stacking transfers representation learning from controlled benchmarks through DEAP and SEED into CHB-MIT clinical windows, and a zero-initialized multi-scale attention adapter delivers event-aligned saliency without destroying stacked features at initialization.

MRANC advances EEG preprocessing from opaque noise suppression toward accountable, stem-resolved signal explanation. We anticipate impact on methodological research (structured artifact modeling) and translational pipelines (ICU monitoring, ambulatory epilepsy care, and multi-center tele-EEG), where clinicians must see not only cleaner traces, but also what was removed, when, and why.
